Seedcoms: Japanese supplements in soft pouches

Published 12 October 2026 · Updated 12 October 2026 · 7 min read · OASIS IKIGAI editorial team

Seedcoms is a Japanese supplement brand sold in light soft pouches instead of jars. Here are the ranges we stock, how to work out how long a pouch lasts and what not to combine.

Seedcoms is a Japanese food supplement brand sold in light, soft stand-up pouches with a zip-lock instead of plastic jars. Its range covers beauty, energy, evening balance and eyes, usually 90 tablets or capsules per pouch. Choose for one or two specific goals and read the ingredients carefully: some formulas contain herbal extracts with important interactions.

Who Seedcoms are and why the packaging

Seedcoms (シードコムス) is a Japanese supplement brand familiar to shoppers on Japanese online stores, recognisable by its minimalist stand-up pouches with a zip-lock.

Why a pouch rather than a jar: it is light, so cheaper to ship, including internationally; it uses less plastic; it is compact and shrinks as you use it; and the opaque film with a zip-lock protects the contents from light and moisture if you close it properly.

Ginseng, maca, cordyceps: what is known about the ingredients

Ginseng (Panax ginseng) is one of the most studied plants in East Asian traditional medicine. NCCIH notes that research on its effects on physical and mental performance, immunity and blood sugar is mixed, and that short-term use at usual doses is well tolerated by most people. Possible side effects include headache, sleep problems and digestive upset, and ginseng may interact with diabetes medicines and anticoagulants.

Maca (Lepidium meyenii) is a root vegetable from the Peruvian highlands, traditionally eaten as food. Research on maca as a supplement is limited and small, with no convincing evidence on energy or libido yet.

Cordyceps (Cordyceps militaris) is a cultivated mushroom; most research has been done in animals or labs.

Marine peptides are protein fragments from marine sources, often fish — important if you have an allergy.

The takeaway: such formulas can be tried as part of an energy ritual, but not as a substitute for sleep, movement and food. If tiredness lasts for weeks, iron deficiency, thyroid or sleep problems may be the cause, so talk to a doctor. On iron, see Heme iron: how it differs from regular iron.

How to read a Seedcoms label

On the back of the pouch: 原材料名 — ingredients; 1日の目安量 — daily serving (for example 1日1〜3粒, 1–⁠3 tablets a day); 内容量 — contents; 賞味期限 — best-before; 保存方法 — storage; ご注意 — precautions. Full cheat sheet: How to read a Japanese supplement label.

How long a pouch lasts. Divide the number of tablets by your daily serving: 90 tablets at 1 a day lasts 90 days, at 3 a day 30 days.

Choosing Seedcoms for your goal

  1. Pick one goal — do not buy five pouches at once.
  2. Check for conflicts — St John's wort (GABA), ginseng (King Power), vitamin D (Cordyceps).
  3. Count duplicates — zinc in King Power, vitamin D in Cordyceps and Rose Supplement.
  4. Start at the lowest serving in the range and assess after a few weeks.

On compatibility, see Which vitamins and minerals should not be taken together. Other Japanese brands: ORIHIRO and DHC.

Best-before and storing the pouch

Close the zip-lock all the way, press out air before closing, store somewhere cool and dry (not the bathroom or a sunny windowsill), do not leave it open near the cooker, and check the 賞味期限 so you finish the pouch before it.

FAQ

Why are Seedcoms supplements sold in pouches rather than jars?

A pouch is lighter, takes up less space and uses less plastic; the opaque film and zip-lock protect the contents if you close it tightly.

How many days does a Seedcoms pouch last?

Divide the number of tablets by your daily serving: 90 tablets at 1 a day lasts 90 days, at 3 a day 30 days.

Can I combine several Seedcoms supplements?

Yes, if they do not duplicate ingredients or conflict with your medicines. Check zinc, vitamin D and herbal extracts.

Is Seedcoms a Japanese brand?

Yes, a Japanese brand; the pouches carry Japanese labelling with ingredients, maker and best-before date.
