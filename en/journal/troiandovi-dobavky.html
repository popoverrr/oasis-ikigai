Rose supplements: the Japanese trend in fragrant self-care

Published 15 October 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026 · 6 min read · OASIS IKIGAI editorial team

Capsules with Damask rose oil — “edible fragrance” — are popular in Japan. Here is what is inside, what to honestly expect and why the sense of scent is so individual.

Rose supplements are a Japanese beauty trend: capsules with Damask rose oil taken as part of a “fragrance from within” ritual. Some people notice a faint floral note on their breath or skin, others notice nothing — it is a subjective impression with no guarantees. Besides rose, such formulas often contain plant oils and fat-soluble vitamins, so it pays to read the ingredients carefully.

What "edible fragrance" means

Japan has a whole category of products called “edible fragrance” or “perfume you drink” (飲む香水): capsules or sweets with essential oils and flower extracts, most often rose. The idea is poetic: instead of applying scent to the skin, you “wear” it from within.

The trend reflects Japanese culture of discretion: in many settings — offices, public transport, restaurants — strong perfume is considered out of place, while a barely-there natural scent feels more appropriate. It is also about ritual: a rose capsule is a small pleasant act in the morning, like a cup of fragrant tea.

It is above all an aesthetic, emotional product, not a hygiene product. It does not replace brushing your teeth, showering or deodorant, and body odour as a medical symptom is something to discuss with a doctor.

How it works: honest expectations

There is no convincing scientific evidence for a “fragrance from within” effect:

  • aromatic compounds from essential oils may partly be released through breath and skin, but the amount and how noticeable it is vary greatly;
  • everything affects the result: diet, coffee, garlic, smoking, hydration and your own skin scent;
  • many people value the ritual itself — a pleasant habit and a feeling of self-care.

So the sense of scent is subjective, and no one can guarantee it.

How to take it

  • 1 capsule a day with water, at the same time each day;
  • with food that contains fat, so the fat-soluble vitamins are absorbed better;
  • judge it after several weeks;
  • do not increase the dose to “smell stronger” — too much vitamin A and D is harmful.

To fit it into a routine, see When to take vitamins: morning or evening.

Who will enjoy it

People who love fragrant rituals; those looking for a subtle alternative to bold perfume in daily life; fans of Japanese aesthetics of small pleasures; and as a gift for beauty lovers.

Take care or consult a doctor: pregnancy and breastfeeding — because of vitamin A (the NHS warns that too much vitamin A in pregnancy can harm the baby); allergy to rose or other plants in the formula; anticoagulants or separate vitamin A, D or E supplements; and regular medication.

A rose ritual: what to add

  • Morning: a capsule with breakfast, a glass of water, light cream and SPF.
  • Evening: a warm bath or shower, a sheet mask, a favourite scent in the room.
  • Skincare: moisture and ceramides — see Ceramide supplements.
  • Beauty from within: collagen or elastin — see Elastin supplements.

Do not overload the ritual: two or three supplements you enjoy are better than a long list. More in the Beauty collection and on the Seedcoms brand page. On the Japanese approach to beauty, see Beauty from within.

Important

This article is for information only and does not replace medical advice. A rose supplement is a food supplement, not a perfume or hygiene product; any sense of scent is subjective and not guaranteed. The formula contains vitamins A, D and E: if you are pregnant, breastfeeding, take medication or other vitamin supplements, talk to your doctor.

FAQ

Does your body really smell of roses?

It is subjective: some people notice a faint floral note, others do not. There is no scientific evidence that guarantees it.

How many capsules a day?

One capsule a day with water, preferably with food. Do not increase the dose: the formula contains vitamins A and D.

Can I take it during pregnancy?

Not without a doctor. It contains vitamin A, and too much in pregnancy can be harmful.

Does a rose supplement replace perfume or deodorant?

No. It is a beauty ritual, not a hygiene product. Worrying changes in body odour are a reason to see a doctor.

Can I combine it with collagen?

Yes, as long as vitamins in different supplements do not overlap.
