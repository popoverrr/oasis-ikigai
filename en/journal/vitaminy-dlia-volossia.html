Vitamins for hair and nails: what really matters

Published 15 October 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026 · 6 min read · OASIS IKIGAI editorial team

Hair loss is rarely caused by a lack of “hair vitamins”. Here are the most common causes, what to check in blood tests and why unnecessary biotin can even get in the way.

Which vitamins does hair need? Mostly the same ones as the rest of the body: if your diet is varied and your blood tests are normal, special “hair vitamins” will change little. The most common causes of hair loss are stress or illness a few months earlier, iron deficiency, thyroid problems and genetics. So start with tests and advice, not a jar of biotin.

Why hair falls out: the most common causes

We lose dozens of hairs every day as part of the normal growth cycle. The American Academy of Dermatology distinguishes hair shedding — temporary, when more hairs than usual enter the resting phase and fall out — from hair loss, when something stops hair growing.

Common causes cited by the AAD and NHS:

  • stress, illness, fever, surgery, childbirth or rapid weight loss — hair starts shedding 2–⁠3 months later and usually recovers on its own;
  • iron deficiency — especially in women with heavy periods and in vegetarians;
  • thyroid problems;
  • hereditary (androgenetic) hair loss;
  • medicines;
  • tight hairstyles, chemical treatments and heat styling.

There are no “hair vitamins” on this list — which is why supplements taken without finding the cause often disappoint.

Iron, zinc, vitamin D: what to check

Discuss with your doctor: a full blood count and ferritin for iron deficiency (see Heme iron: how it differs from regular iron); TSH for the thyroid; vitamin D (25(OH)D), especially after winter; and zinc, B12 or hormones if needed.

Iron and zinc supplements make sense when tests show a shortfall: too much iron is harmful, and long-term high-dose zinc reduces copper absorption. On zinc, see Zinc: why the body needs it and how to take it.

Biotin: myths

Biotin (vitamin B7) is the most popular “hair vitamin”. Facts from NIH:

  • biotin deficiency is rare — ordinary food provides enough;
  • there is no convincing evidence that biotin supplements improve hair or nails in people who are not deficient;
  • high doses of biotin can distort laboratory results, including thyroid hormones and troponin. If you take biotin, tell your doctor before any tests.

“Biotin for hair” is mostly marketing. A normal dose in a multivitamin is fine; separate megadoses without need are not.

Scalp massage

Scalp massage is a pleasant ritual common in Japanese care too. It feels comfortable, helps you relax and spreads care products evenly. A few small studies link regular massage to a feeling of thicker hair, but the evidence is weak, and massage does not treat alopecia. Use your fingertips while washing or a brush — gently, without pressing or rubbing.

When to see a trichologist

See a trichologist or dermatologist if hair comes out in clumps or bald patches appear; shedding lasts more than a few months; your scalp itches, reddens, flakes or hurts; you have other symptoms such as tiredness, feeling cold, weight change or cycle changes; or hereditary hair loss runs in your family. The NHS notes that some types of hair loss respond well to treatment if it starts early.

Nails: a separate note

Brittle nails are more often linked to frequent contact with water and detergents, dry air, nail polish and removing gel polish than to vitamin shortages. Gloves for cleaning, daily hand and cuticle cream and breaks between manicures help. But changes in shape or colour, or spoon-shaped nails, can signal iron deficiency — see a doctor.

Important

This article is for information only and does not replace medical advice. Food supplements do not treat hair loss. Take iron only after blood tests, and tell your doctor about biotin before any tests. If you have patchy hair loss, long-lasting shedding or scalp changes, see a dermatologist.

FAQ

Which tests should I have for hair loss?

Doctors most often suggest a full blood count, ferritin, TSH and vitamin D, plus others if needed.

Does zinc help hair?

If you are low in zinc, correcting it helps. If you have enough, extra doses do not make hair thicker.

How long until I see results?

Hair grows slowly: judge changes 3–⁠6 months after the cause has been addressed.

Does biotin help with hair loss?

Without a biotin deficiency there is no convincing evidence, and high doses can distort test results.

Can I massage my scalp every day?

Yes, gentle massage with fingertips or a brush is fine daily, without pressing hard.
