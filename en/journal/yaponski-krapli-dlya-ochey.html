Japanese eye drops: how to choose and how they differ

Published 30 September 2026 · OASIS IKIGAI editorial team

Japanese eye drops are popular for moisture and a fresh feeling. Here is how to choose them, what the marks on the pack mean and when drops cannot replace a doctor.

People choose Japanese eye drops for moisture and a fresh feeling when their eyes are tired from screens, air conditioning or contact lenses. Before buying, read the purpose, age and contact-lens instructions on the pack — and if you have pain, redness or worsening vision, see an eye doctor rather than reaching for drops.

Why Japanese eye drops are so popular

In Japan, eye drops are an everyday item in the bag, like lip balm. People spend a lot of time at screens, and makers offer many variants: moisturising, cooling, for contact-lens wearers. Japanese bottles are convenient, with a precise dropper, and the pack explains in detail what they are for and how to use them.

What eye drops are for

Broadly, Japanese eye drops fall into several groups by the purpose stated on the pack:

  • Moisturising — for a dry feeling, for example after long computer work.
  • Refreshing with a cooling effect — for a fresh sensation; the level of “coolness” is often shown on a scale on the box.
  • For contact lenses — compatible with certain lens types, which are stated on the pack.
  • For tired eyes — for those who read a lot or work at a screen.

Always check the exact purpose and ingredients on the pack of the specific bottle.

How to read the pack of Japanese eye drops

A Japanese box usually shows:

  • purpose — moisture, tiredness, freshness;
  • directions — how many drops and how many times a day;
  • age restriction — often the age from which the drops may be used;
  • contact-lens compatibility — a separate line or icon;
  • expiry date and how many days to use the bottle after opening.

General rules for reading a Japanese label are covered in How to read a Japanese supplement label.

Eye drops and children: the age on the pack

Many Japanese eye drops state a minimum age — for example, from 7 or from 15 years. This is not a formality: children's eyes are more sensitive, and adult drops are formulated for adults.

  • For children — only drops whose label clearly states they are suitable for children of that age.
  • Even then — after consulting an eye doctor or paediatrician.
  • If a child complains of tired eyes, start with screen habits and breaks: see Eyes and screens: protecting a schoolchild's vision.

When drops will not help and you need a doctor

Eye drops are meant for mild discomfort, not for treating disease. See an eye doctor immediately if:

  • your eyes hurt;
  • redness lasts more than two days;
  • your vision has worsened, or you see double or a “fog”;
  • there is discharge, swollen eyelids or sensitivity to light;
  • a foreign body or chemical has got into your eye.

The 20-20-20 rule and habits that protect your eyes

The American Academy of Pediatrics suggests the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes at a screen, look at something 20 feet (about 6 metres) away for 20 seconds. It is useful for adults too. A few more habits:

  • keep the screen at or slightly below eye level;
  • match screen brightness to the room lighting, without glare from the window;
  • remind yourself to blink more when looking at a screen for a long time;
  • air the room and humidify the air in the heating season.

How to use eye drops correctly

  • Wash your hands before using drops.
  • Do not touch your eye or lashes with the tip of the bottle.
  • Do not share drops with others.
  • Follow the number of drops and times per day on the pack.
  • If you wear lenses, check compatibility or remove the lenses before using drops.
  • Throw the bottle away after the period stated for an opened pack.

FAQ

Can children use Japanese eye drops?

Only drops whose label clearly states they are suitable for children of that age, and only after consulting an eye doctor or paediatrician. Many Japanese eye drops are only for ages 7+ or 15+.

Can I use drops while wearing contact lenses?

Only if the pack states compatibility with your type of lenses. Otherwise, remove the lenses first.

When should I see a doctor instead of using drops?

If your eyes hurt, redness lasts more than two days, your vision worsens or there is discharge — do not wait, see an eye doctor.

Do lutein and bilberry help?

They are food supplements considered part of the diet. They do not treat eye diseases or replace an eye examination.
